# Dimension 1: Global Smart City Indexes & Ranking Platforms — Research Findings

## Executive Summary

This document presents comprehensive research on the world's leading smart city indexes and ranking platforms, analyzing their methodologies, visual design approaches, content depth, and unique features. The analysis covers six major global indexes: IESE Cities in Motion Index (Barcelona), IMD Smart City Index (Singapore/Lausanne), Copenhagenize Index (Copenhagen), EIU Global Liveability Index (London), Forbes World's Best Smart Cities, and additional award-winning smart city data platforms. For each, we identify content gaps relative to Thailand's SCITI (Smart City Thailand Index) and extract actionable insights for platform enhancement.

**Research conducted:** 12 web searches across 30+ sources | **Date:** July 2025

## 1. IESE Cities in Motion Index (CIMI) — Barcelona, Spain

### Overview
The IESE Cities in Motion Index (CIMI) is published by IESE Business School at the University of Navarra, Spain. It is one of the most comprehensive and academically rigorous city indexes globally, covering **183 cities across 92 countries** in its 2026 edition [^23^][^28^].

### Methodology

**Dimensions:** 9 core dimensions (evolved from an original 10) [^13^][^19^]:
1. **Economy** (weight: 1.0 — highest priority)
2. **Human Capital** (weight: 0.392)
3. **International Profile** (weight: 0.581)
4. **Urban Planning** (weight: 0.575)
5. **Environment** (weight: 0.386)
6. **Technology** (weight: 0.615)
7. **Governance** (weight: 0.714)
8. **Social Cohesion** (weight: 0.592)
9. **Mobility and Transportation** (weight: 0.473)

**Statistical Method:** Uses the internationally recognized **DP2 technique** (distance-based aggregation), which quantifies the deviation of each indicator's value from a reference point and addresses interdependence among sub-indicators to avoid over-sensitivity [^13^][^14^]. This is significantly more sophisticated than simple weighted averages used by other indexes.

**Indicators:** Over **100 statistical indicators** per city, combining objective data (e.g., GDP, broadband subscriptions, metro stations) with subjective data (e.g., purchasing power, women's development indicators) [^83^][^13^].

**Key indicators include:**
- Technology: Broadband subscriptions, wireless access points, Innovation Cities Index, Apple Store presence
- Mobility: Bike-sharing systems, commute time index, traffic index, metro network length, high-speed rail availability
- Environment: Green spaces, air quality, climate change policies
- International Profile: Airport passengers, ICCA congress meetings, hotel availability

### Visual Design & Interactive Features

**Radar Charts (Signature Visual):** CIMI's most distinctive visual feature is its **interactive radar charts** profiling all 183 cities across the 9 dimensions [^14^][^87^]. These enable intuitive comparison of two or more cities at a glance. Available at `citiesinmotion.iese.edu` with an interactive interface [^83^].

**Performance Coverage Area Indicator:** Introduced in recent editions, this indicator shows both current performance and growth potential. It represents the area covered within a nonagon (9-sided polygon) — a perfect city would fill 100% [^87^]. For example, London's coverage area of 73% means a 27% growth margin to theoretical perfection.

**Heat-Mapped Rankings Tables:** Darker greens represent highest positions; darker reds represent least favorable; yellow shades for intermediate positions [^15^].

**Cluster Analysis (2024+):** Introduced statistical cluster analysis identifying **6 distinct groups of cities**, each with unique combinations of technology, green infrastructure, and labor markets — providing an alternative perspective beyond traditional ranking [^13^].

**City Comparison Tool:** Interactive website allows users to compare two cities simultaneously side-by-side [^14^][^83^].

### Content Depth & Types
- **Full PDF report** (~130+ pages) with complete methodology, rankings by dimension, and city profiles
- **Special city analyses** (e.g., London deep-dive highlighting leadership and improvement areas) [^13^]
- **Ranking by dimension tables** showing each city's position across all 9 dimensions
- **Graphical appendix** with radar charts for all 183 cities
- **Sensitivity studies** demonstrating methodological robustness
- **Data transparency:** Explicit acknowledgment of limitations (e.g., national-level data used as city proxies, war-affected cities like Kyiv) [^13^]

### What Makes It Credible & Award-Worthy
- **Academic rigor:** DP2 statistical methodology published in peer-reviewed frameworks
- **Longevity:** Annual editions since 2014, providing time-series comparability
- **Transparency:** Full methodology documentation, sensitivity analyses, explicit limitation statements
- **Breadth:** 183 cities across 92 countries — among the widest geographical coverage of any city index
- **Balance:** Combines objective metrics (100+ indicators) with subjective assessments
- **Evolution:** Regularly adds indicators (e.g., women's development, hourly wages) and refines methodology [^83^]

### Content Gaps SCITI Might Have Relative to CIMI
| CIMI Feature | SCITI Potential Gap |
|---|---|
| 100+ objective indicators across 9 dimensions | May lack comparable depth of quantitative indicators |
| Interactive radar charts for all cities | May lack interactive visual comparison tools |
| Performance coverage area / growth potential indicator | May not show growth trajectory or improvement potential |
| Cluster analysis identifying city archetypes | May lack city segmentation/grouping analysis |
| Academic DP2 statistical methodology | May lack published, peer-reviewed methodology documentation |
| Special case studies on top-performing cities | May lack in-depth city spotlight analyses |
| Sensitivity studies and explicit limitation notes | May lack transparency about data limitations |
| 9-year+ time series for trend analysis | May lack longitudinal comparative data |

## 2. IMD Smart City Index — Lausanne, Switzerland / Singapore

### Overview
Published by the Institute for Management Development (IMD) World Competitiveness Center in partnership with the Singapore University of Technology and Design (SUTD), the IMD Smart City Index is now in its **7th edition (2025)**, ranking **146 cities worldwide** [^25^][^27^]. Singapore topped the ranking for three consecutive years [^103^].

### What Makes It Award-Winning & Unique

**Resident-Centric Perception Methodology:** Unlike data-heavy indexes, IMD's defining innovation is its **citizen perception-based approach**. Data is collected from approximately **120 residents per city** who respond to questions about their lived experience of urban technology and infrastructure [^22^][^103^]. This makes it uniquely people-centered.

**Two-Pillar Framework:** [^22^][^30^]
- **Structures Pillar:** Quality of existing physical infrastructure
- **Technology Pillar:** Availability and effectiveness of technological solutions

Each pillar is evaluated across **5 key areas:**
1. Health and Safety
2. Mobility
3. Activities (cultural/social)
4. Opportunities (work/education)
5. Governance

**Temporal Smoothing:** Uses a **3-year weighted average** (3:2:1 ratio for 2024:2023:2021) to reduce variability from one-off events and provide stable trend analysis [^22^][^30^]. This is a significant methodological innovation.

**HDI-Based Grouping:** Cities are categorized into **4 groups based on Human Development Index (HDI)** scores, ensuring fair comparisons among cities with similar development levels rather than pitting Zurich against Nairobi directly [^22^][^30^].

**Dual Rating Format:** Results presented as both an overall ranking (1-146) AND individual pillar ratings with letter grades (AAA to D) within each HDI group [^30^].

### Visual Design & User Experience

**City Profile Pages:** Each of the 146 cities gets a detailed profile page including [^30^]:
- Smart City Ranking position
- Factor ratings (letter grades for Structures and Technologies)
- HDI group classification
- Background information (city-level HDI, population, map location)
- **Priority Areas chart:** Visual bar chart showing what residents perceive as most urgent for their city (selected from 15 indicators)
- **Attitudes section:** Blue bar charts comparing city vs. group average on privacy attitudes, facial recognition comfort, trust in authorities, and cashless transactions
- **Structures and Technologies comparison table:** Side-by-side visual comparison across all 5 key areas with color-coded scoring

**Priority Areas Visualization:** This is a particularly strong UX feature — from a list of 15 areas (affordable housing, health services, air quality, etc.), survey respondents select their top 5 priorities. Results displayed as horizontal bar charts showing percentage of respondents, immediately revealing citizen concerns [^30^].

**Rating Scale Visuals:** Letter-grade ratings (AAA, AA, A, BBB, BB, B, CCC, CC, C, D) provide intuitive, educationally familiar performance classification within each HDI peer group [^30^].

### Content Types
- **Free downloadable PDF report** (~100+ pages) with full rankings and methodology
- **Individual city profiles** with visual data presentations
- **Priority areas analysis** revealing citizen concerns globally (affordable housing emerged as #1 concern worldwide in recent editions) [^103^]
- **Attitudes section** tracking privacy, trust, and digital payment trends
- **Time series analysis** enabling 3-year moving average comparisons
- **City spotlights** and thematic analysis (e.g., housing affordability as technology challenge) [^25^]
- **Partnership with WeGO** (World Smart Sustainable Cities Organization) for city leader engagement [^30^]

### What Makes It Credible & Award-Worthy
- **Resident-centric authenticity:** Captures lived experience, not just infrastructure counts
- **Methodological stability:** 7 years of data with temporal smoothing enables genuine trend analysis
- **Context-sensitive comparison:** HDI grouping prevents unfair cross-development comparisons
- **Academic partnership:** Collaboration between IMD (top business school) and SUTD
- **Policy relevance:** Explicitly designed as "a tool for action" for urban policy leaders [^30^]
- **Global media recognition:** Widely cited by Bloomberg, Forbes, Strait Times, and global media [^103^]

### Content Gaps SCITI Might Have Relative to IMD SCI
| IMD SCI Feature | SCITI Potential Gap |
|---|---|
| Citizen perception surveys (120 residents/city) | May lack direct citizen satisfaction/perception data |
| 3-year weighted moving average | May not have multi-year time series with smoothing |
| HDI-based peer grouping for fair comparison | May lack development-context-adjusted comparison framework |
| Priority Areas visualization (citizen-selected concerns) | May not have citizen-prioritized issue identification |
| Attitudes tracking (privacy, trust, digital payments) | May lack citizen attitude/behavioral trend data |
| Individual city profile pages with visual data | May lack detailed individual city profile pages |
| Letter-grade rating system within peer groups | May lack intuitive rating/grading system |
| Partnership with academic institution (SUTD) | May lack formal academic partnership for credibility |

## 3. Copenhagenize Index — Copenhagen, Denmark

### Overview
The Copenhagenize Index, started in 2011 and now in its 2025 edition (EIT Urban Mobility Edition), is the **world's most comprehensive inventory and ranking of bicycle-friendly cities** [^17^][^16^]. It evaluates 100 cities globally, with the Top 30 receiving detailed analysis [^17^].

### Methodology

**3 Pillars, 13 Indicators:** [^10^][^17^]

**Pillar 1: Safe & Connected Infrastructure**
1. Bicycle Infrastructure (km of protected lanes per 100 km roadway)
2. Bicycle Parking & Services
3. Public Transport Integration
4. Traffic Calming & Safety

**Pillar 2: Usage & Reach**
5. Cycling Modal Share
6. Gender Split (female cycling participation)
7. Cargo Bike Adoption
8. Bike-Sharing Systems

**Pillar 3: Policy & Support**
9. Cycling Master Plan / SUMP
10. Cycling Budget & Investment
11. Design Standards & Guidelines
12. Cycling Team / Institutional Capacity
13. Data Collection & Monitoring

**Scoring:** All indicators standardized to 0-100 scale. Quantitative indicators use min-max normalization; qualitative indicators use predefined point bands. Composite score is a **simple average of 13 indicator scores** with equal weighting [^17^].

**2025 Top 5:** Utrecht (#1), Copenhagen (#2), Ghent (#3), Amsterdam (#4), Paris (#5) [^18^]

### Visual Design & Content Approach

**Full-Color Report (100+ pages):** Professional PDF report with rich visual design including:
- Data-driven infographics and statistics highlights
- Individual city pages with overall score, pillar scores, and narrative analysis
- **"The Way Forward" sections** for each city providing specific policy recommendations [^17^]
- Success stories section highlighting best practices
- Global insights with key statistics (e.g., "90% of Top 30 cities regularly monitor cycling progress") [^17^]

**Narrative City Profiles:** Each Top 30 city receives a multi-paragraph narrative analysis that goes beyond numbers to tell the city's cycling story, identify challenges, and suggest "The Way Forward" [^17^]. Example for Bonn: discusses modal share growth from 15% (2017) to 21% (2024), infrastructure investments, and specific recommendations.

**Bicycle-Friendly City Benchmark Report (Paid Service):** A **16-page illustrated tailored report** comparing a city's performance against 5 carefully selected peer cities plus Top 30 and regional averages [^33^]. Delivered within 15 working days. Price: EUR 2,800-4,800.

**Paid Implementation Support Options:** [^33^]
- Facilitated capacity-building workshops with political/technical teams
- Follow-up advisory support meetings
- Detailed 30-40 page Bicycle Policy Evaluation & Benchmark Report
- Strategic presentation to elected officials or city leadership (EUR 500)

**Training & Master Classes:** [^54^]
- Essentials Master Class (in-person or hybrid)
- Customized editions for single entities
- Online condensed versions for NGOs
- Topics: Cycling Strategy, Infrastructure Design, Bikeconomics, Equity & Inclusion, Communication & Advocacy

### What Makes It Credible & Award-Worthy
- **13-year track record** (since 2011) demonstrating consistency and evolution
- **Evidence-based, data-driven framework** balancing quantitative and qualitative indicators
- **EIT Urban Mobility partnership** (EU-funded innovation partnership) for 2025 edition [^17^]
- **Diagnostic tool, not just ranking:** Positions user (cyclist) at heart of assessment
- **Systems-based approach:** Links physical design, policy commitment, and user experience [^10^]
- **Consulting + index integration:** Turns insights into actionable policy recommendations
- **Peer comparison methodology:** Benchmarks against comparable cities, not just top performers

### Content Gaps SCITI Might Have Relative to Copenhagenize Index
| Copenhagenize Feature | SCITI Potential Gap |
|---|---|
| "The Way Forward" policy recommendations per city | May lack specific policy action recommendations |
| Paid benchmark reports for city governments | May not offer tailored benchmarking services |
| Capacity-building workshops and master classes | May lack training/education programs for city officials |
| Implementation support (advisory, presentations) | May not provide implementation consulting |
| Success stories / best practices section | May lack curated best practices case studies |
| 13-indicator equal-weight transparent scoring | May lack equally transparent, detailed indicator breakdown |
| Multi-paragraph narrative city profiles | May lack narrative storytelling beyond data tables |
| Integration with EU-funded mobility innovation network | May lack connection to international innovation partnerships |

## 4. EIU Global Liveability Index — London, UK

### Overview
Published by the Economist Intelligence Unit (EIU), the Global Liveability Index is one of the **most established and widely referenced city benchmarks globally**. The 2025 edition ranks **173 cities** across five categories [^51^][^57^]. First launched in 2004, it has over 20 years of historical data.

### Methodology

**5 Categories with 30+ Indicators:** [^12^][^51^]

| Category | Weight | Key Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| **Stability** | 25% | Petty crime, violent crime, terror threat, military conflict threat, civil unrest |
| **Healthcare** | 20% | Private/public healthcare availability & quality, OTC drugs, World Bank health indicators |
| **Culture & Environment** | 25% | Climate, corruption, religious/social restrictions, censorship, sporting/cultural availability, food & drink |
| **Education** | 10% | Public education indicators |
| **Infrastructure** | 20% | Quality of road network, public transport, international links, energy, water, telecommunications |

**Rating Scale:** Each indicator rated on a 5-point qualitative scale [^12^]:
- Acceptable (ideal conditions)
- Tolerable
- Uncomfortable
- Undesirable
- Intolerable

**Scoring:** Weighted compilation producing a score from **1 (intolerable) to 100 (ideal)** [^12^].

**2025 Top 10:** Copenhagen (#1), Vienna (#2), Zurich (#2), Melbourne (#4), Geneva (#5), Sydney (#6), Osaka (#7), Auckland (#7), Adelaide (#9), Vancouver (#10) [^56^][^51^]

### Visual Design & Content Approach

**Free Summary Report:** EIU publishes a free annual summary report with full rankings tables, category breakdowns, and thematic analysis [^51^][^53^].

**Category Score Breakdown Tables:** Rankings presented with scores across all 5 categories, enabling multi-dimensional comparison [^56^]. Example: Copenhagen scored 100.0 for Stability, Education, and Infrastructure; 95.8 for Healthcare; 95.4 for Culture & Environment.

**"Biggest Movers" Analysis:** Dedicated sections tracking biggest risers and fallers with explanatory narrative [^51^]. Example: Al Khobar (Saudi Arabia) climbed 13 places due to Vision 2030 healthcare/education investments; Calgary fell 13 places due to healthcare service strains.

**Regional Analysis:** Thematic sections analyzing performance by region (Western Europe, North America, Asia-Pacific, Middle East & Africa) [^51^].

**Content Hub & Virtual Events:** EIU maintains an ongoing **Global Liveability Index content hub** with in-depth analysis, expert commentary, data-led insights, and virtual events featuring index authors discussing findings [^53^][^55^].

**Liveability Scale for Expats:** Practical application table correlating scores to recommended hardship allowances [^12^]:
- 80-100: No challenges (0% allowance)
- 70-80: Generally fine with some problems (5%)
- 60-70: Negative factors impact living (10%)
- 50-60: Substantially constrained (15%)
- Below 50: Severely restricted (20%)

### Content Types
- **Annual summary report** (free download)
- **Full customizable datasets** (paid product)
- **Detailed city profiles** (paid product)
- **Virtual events** with expert discussions
- **Ongoing content hub** with expert commentary throughout the year
- **Regional trend analyses**
- **Movers and shakers tracking** with explanatory journalism

### What Makes It Credible & Award-Worthy
- **20+ year track record** (since 2004) — the longest-running liveability index
- **EIU brand authority:** Published by The Economist's intelligence arm, one of the world's most respected analytical organizations
- **Global media dominance:** Universally cited by BBC, CNN, Reuters, and every major news outlet
- **Practical applicability:** Used by multinational corporations for expatriate relocation packages
- **In-house expert network:** Ratings based on judgment of in-house country analysts and field correspondents in each city [^12^]
- **Transparent methodology:** Clear category weights and rating criteria publicly documented
- **Annual virtual events:** Index authors discuss findings publicly, adding accountability

### Content Gaps SCITI Might Have Relative to EIU
| EIU Feature | SCITI Potential Gap |
|---|---|
| 20+ year time series (since 2004) | May lack longitudinal historical data |
| 5-point qualitative rating scale (acceptable to intolerable) | May lack qualitative rating framework |
| "Biggest movers" analysis with narrative explanations | May not track and explain ranking changes |
| Regional analysis sections | May lack regional comparative analysis |
| Virtual events with index authors | May lack public-facing expert events |
| Ongoing content hub beyond annual report | May not have year-round content updates |
| Practical applicability (expat allowance guidance) | May lack practical application guidance for businesses |
| Customizable datasets (paid premium product) | May not offer premium data products |

## 5. Forbes World's Best Smart Cities / Other Rankings

Forbes has published smart city rankings evaluating global urban centers. The **2025 Forbes ranking** evaluated cities across **5 key criteria**: smart governance, living standards, mobility, business and employment, and sustainable development [^20^].

### 2025 Forbes Top 5

3. **Beijing** (52.9 points) — AI focus, smart mobility, urban sustainability

### Other Notable Rankings

- Analyzes **95 cities** across Europe and the US
- **17 equally-weighted metrics** in 3 categories: connectivity/infrastructure, sustainability, tech job market
- 2025 Top European city: Paris; Top US city: Atlanta
- Partnership with **Ookla for Mobile 5G performance data**

## 6. Award-Winning Smart City Dashboards & Data Platforms

### 6.1 LORDIMAS (Local and Regional Digital Maturity Assessment) — EU

**The most relevant award-winning smart city assessment platform for SCITI to learn from.**

**Overview:** LORDIMAS is a **free, interactive digital maturity assessment tool** developed by ESPON in cooperation with the European Committee of the Regions, European Commission, and Living-in.EU [^97^][^98^]. Over **130 European cities and regions** have used it.

**7 Dimensions of Assessment:** [^97^][^99^]
1. Governance
2. Service Design
3. Data Management
4. Interoperability
5. Service Delivery
6. Technology
7. Networking

**Interactive Dashboard Features:** [^97^][^98^]
- **Real-time results visualization** after completing assessment
- **Peer comparison** — compare results with other cities and regions across Europe
- **Interactive dashboard** with 29 digital indicators across 7 sections
- **Six maturity levels** from "digitally nascent" to "digitally native"
- **Annual monitoring** capability to track progress over time
- **Tailored policy recommendations** based on assessment results
- **Best practice examples** integrated into each dimension report

**Core Functionalities:** [^97^]
- **Evaluate** — Assess digital maturity via 30-minute questionnaire
- **Compare** — Real-time interactive dashboard peer comparison
- **Share** — Promote success and challenges
- **Learn** — Get policy recommendations for emerging topics

**Best Practice Integration:** Each dimension report includes specific city best practices [^99^]:
- Terrassa (Italy): "Participa a Terrassa" citizen engagement platform
- Ghent (Belgium): Proactive service delivery auto-granting social benefits
- Amsterdam (Netherlands): Public sensor registry for transparency
- Helsinki (Finland): Forum Virium innovation agency
- Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia: IoT and AI technology leadership

**Maturity Score Distribution:** Range from 8 to 95, with median of 49 ("digitally emerging"). Top performers include Rivas Vaciamadrid, Espoo, Arteixo [^99^].

**Procurement Support Connection:** Cities completing LORDIMAS can access fully-funded consultancy services under the Digital Europe Programme, including procurement guidelines and templates compliant with open standards [^102^].

### 6.2 City Dashboard Examples

**Cork Dashboard (Ireland):** [^34^]
- Real-time information, time-series indicator data, interactive maps
- Serves citizens, public sector workers, and companies

**Barcelona Smart City Dashboard:** [^81^]
- GIS-based open-source dashboard using Apache Superset
- Air quality parameters mapped against traffic density
- Multiple visualization types: line graphs, box plots, heatmaps, scatter plots

**Dubai Live Platform (2025):** [^85^]
- City-wide, AI-powered command hub integrating real-time monitoring, predictive analytics, digital-twin modeling
- Cross-agency coordination across mobility, infrastructure, waste, inspections
- Anomaly detection and automated response

**Suresnes AI-Assisted Contact Centre (France):** [^85^]
- 24/7 citizen service via chatbots, voicebots, callbots
- 60% of inquiries resolved at first contact
- 40% reduction in escalations to human agents
- Centralized knowledge base for consistent responses

### 6.3 Smart City Open Data Visualization Approaches

Research on big urban data visualization for smart cities identifies key techniques used in leading dashboards [^81^][^82^]:

**Visualization Types Used:**
- **Interactive maps** (Leaflet, GIS integration)
- **Time-series plots** with smoothed lines and median baselines
- **Correlation heatmaps** showing interrelationships between urban variables
- **Box plots** for statistical ranges and variability
- **Stacked bar charts** for proportional distributions
- **Drill-down dashboards** for exploring data layers

**Key Dashboard Features:**
- Fixed header with persistent navigation
- Color-coded sensor locations on interactive maps
- User-driven filtering to isolate specific variables
- Reorganizable, resizable data panels
- Responsive layout for desktop, tablet, and mobile
- AJAX-based real-time updates without page reloads
- Privacy-aware design aligned with ethical data practices

## 8. Thailand's SCITI Context

### Current SCITI Framework

Thailand's Smart City Thailand Index (SCITI) evaluates certified smart cities across **7 dimensions** aligned with Thailand's smart city development framework [^104^][^105^]:

1. **Smart Environment** — Air/water quality, waste management, CO2 reduction >=1% annually
2. **Smart Energy** — Energy efficiency, clean energy adoption >=1% annually
3. **Smart Mobility** — Traffic/transportation systems, connectedness
4. **Smart Economy** — Digital technology for value-addedness, smart agriculture/tourism
5. **Smart Living** — Livability index (health, safety) >=80% annually
6. **Smart People** — Information access >=60%, citizen engagement >=60%
7. **Smart Governance** — Digital governance, data platforms

**Key evaluation criteria** include geographic boundary definition, infrastructure development plan, city data platform with cybersecurity, smart solutions across all 7 dimensions, and sustainable management model [^104^].

**Data Readiness Challenges:** Research across Thailand's 36 certified smart cities reveals a "systematic imbalance" — governance demand for data is high (NPI 0.769-0.890 across domains), but actual data availability is critically low (coverage 0.110-0.231) [^105^]. This creates structural readiness gaps, with Smart Mobility (PR=0.793), Smart Living (0.722), and Smart Economy (0.702) being most urgent.
