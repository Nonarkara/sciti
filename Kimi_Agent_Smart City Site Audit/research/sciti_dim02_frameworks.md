# Smart City Frameworks, Standards & Governance Models — Research Report

## Executive Summary

This report analyzes eight leading smart city frameworks and standards from around the world, examining their KPIs, governance models, citizen engagement mechanisms, and how each defines and measures "smart." The frameworks span global initiatives (UN-Habitat CPI, ISO standards), regional networks (ASEAN, EU), and national models (Singapore, South Korea, Japan, Dubai). Together they represent the most comprehensive and influential approaches to smart city development, measurement, and governance globally.

## 1. UN-Habitat City Prosperity Initiative (CPI)

### Overview
The City Prosperity Initiative (CPI) is UN-Habitat's flagship framework for measuring urban prosperity holistically. By 2020, it had been applied to evaluate urban performance in **539 cities across 54 countries** [^38^]. The CPI is both a metric and a policy dialogue tool, designed to help cities identify opportunities for intervention and formulate evidence-based policies aligned with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the New Urban Agenda [^36^].

### Core Dimensions and KPIs

The CPI measures six dimensions of prosperity, metaphorically represented as spokes of a "Wheel of Urban Prosperity" [^37^]:

| Dimension | Sub-Dimensions | Key Indicators |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Productivity** | Economic Strength, Economic Agglomeration, Employment | City product per capita, economic density, unemployment rate, employment-to-population ratio, informal employment |
| **Infrastructure Development** | Housing, Social Infrastructure, ICT, Urban Mobility, Urban Form | Access to improved water/sanitation/electricity, internet access, broadband speed, public transport use, street intersection density, land allocated to streets |
| **Quality of Life** | Health, Education, Safety & Security, Public Space | Life expectancy, under-5 mortality, literacy rate, mean years of schooling, homicide rate, accessibility to open public areas, green area per capita |
| **Equity & Social Inclusion** | Social Inclusion indicators | Gini coefficient, poverty rate, slum households, youth unemployment, equitable school enrollment, women in local government/workforce |
| **Environmental Sustainability** | Urban Development, Air Quality, Waste Management, Sustainable Energy | Land use mix, PM2.5 concentration, CO2 emissions, solid waste collection, wastewater treatment, renewable energy share |
| **Urban Governance & Legislation** | Participation, Municipal Finance, Governance of Urbanization | Voter turnout, access to public information, civic participation, own revenue collection, days to start a business, subnational debt, land use efficiency |

**Total indicators**: 25+ indicators at Basic CPI level; extended versions include additional metrics such as vaccination coverage, maternal mortality, theft rates, and green area per capita [^36^].

### Unique Feature: Spatial Indicators
The CPI innovatively integrates **spatial indicators** to measure urban form — specifically street intersection density, street density, and land allocated to streets. UN-Habitat research shows that "adequate provisioning of streets and public spaces are associated with urban prosperity" and that "more efficient and productive cities, with better quality of life and environmental indicators are often cities with better street connectivity" [^35^].

### Governance Model
- **Top-down with local adaptation**: UN-Habitat provides the methodology, cities implement with technical assistance
- **Three-phase process**: (1) Assessing Urban Prosperity → (2) Urban Prosperity Analysis → (3) Action Plan Definition [^34^]
- **Institutional strategy**: Expected results strengthen institutional coordination toward comprehensive solutions [^34^]

### Citizen Engagement Mechanisms
- **Perception Surveys**: Systematic feedback on how people understand and feel about different dimensions of prosperity
- **City Profile Briefing**: Governments receive feedback facilitating collaboration between government, private sector, and civil society [^34^]
- **Civic participation indicators**: Voter turnout and civic participation are explicit governance metrics [^36^]

### How It Measures "Smart" Differently
The CPI defines prosperity as **shared, balanced development** across all six dimensions — none should prevail over others. It uses a "Wheel of Urban Prosperity" metaphor where uneven spokes indicate policy intervention needs. It is the only framework explicitly designed as a **global monitoring mechanism for SDG 11** (Sustainable Cities), covering all 10 targets of Goal 11 and 23% of all SDG targets measurable at the local level [^34^].

### Referencable Content
- **CPI Toolkit**: Step-by-step implementation guide [^37^]
- **City Prosperity Index Metadata**: Full methodological documentation with indicator definitions, benchmarks, and standardization formulas [^36^]
- **State of the City Report**: Template for detailed assessment
- **City Action Plan**: Template for transformational interventions aligned with the New Urban Agenda [^34^]
- **CPI Data Portal**: Open data for 333+ cities across 25+ indicators [^38^]

## 2. ISO 37120 vs. ISO 37122

### Overview
These complementary ISO standards provide the world's first internationally standardized frameworks for city data. ISO 37120 (2014, updated 2018/2021) establishes baseline indicators for **sustainable city services and quality of life**, while ISO 37122 (2019) adds **smart city-specific indicators** that measure technology deployment and digital maturity [^113^][^81^].

### ISO 37120: Sustainable Cities Indicators

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Published** | 2014 (updated 2018; 2021 edition expanded to 128 KPIs) |
| **Indicators** | 104 KPIs (56 core, 48 supporting) across 19 themes |
| **Focus** | City services and quality of life |
| **Certification** | WCCD Platinum/Gold/Silver levels based on indicators reported |

**19 Themes**: Economy, Education, Energy, Environment, Finance, Governance, Health, Housing, Population & Social Conditions, Recreation, Safety, Solid Waste, Sports & Culture, Telecommunication, Transportation, Urban/Local Agriculture, Urban Planning, Wastewater, Water [^113^][^115^].

**Sample Core Indicators**: City product per capita, unemployment rate, access to improved water/sanitation, particulate matter concentration, number of firefighters per 100,000 population, traffic fatalities per 100,000 population, residential electrical use per capita [^113^].

### ISO 37122: Smart City Indicators

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Published** | 2019 |
| **Indicators** | 81 indicators across 20 sectors |
| **Focus** | Technology deployment, digital services, smart infrastructure |
| **Prerequisite** | ISO 37120 certification recommended |

**20 Sectors with Smart-Specific Indicators** [^81^][^80^]:

| Sector | # of Indicators | Examples |
|--------|----------------|----------|
| **Transportation** | 14 | Real-time traffic alerts coverage, % of intelligent traffic lights, % of autonomous vehicles registered, sharing economy transport users, public transport real-time systems, unified payment systems |
| **Energy** | 10 | Energy from wastewater/solid waste, decentralized electricity production, smart energy meter penetration, EV charging stations per EV, street lighting management systems |
| **Solid Waste** | 6 | Sensor-enabled garbage bins, telemetering at drop-off centers, door-to-door waste monitoring with individual quantity tracking |
| **Water** | 4 | Smart water system coverage, real-time water quality monitoring, smart water meter penetration |
| **Governance** | 4 | Open data portal visits, % city services accessible online, IT infrastructure downtime, non-emergency inquiry response time |
| **Telecommunication** | 3 | Broadband access rate, white zone/dead spot coverage, municipal internet connectivity |
| **Health** | 3 | Online unified health files, remote medical appointments, real-time public alert systems |
| **Safety** | 1 | Digital surveillance camera coverage |
| **Housing** | 2 | Smart energy/water meter household penetration |
| **Urban Planning** | 4 | Citizens engaged in planning process (per 100,000), electronic building permit submission, building permit approval time |
| **Environment** | 3 | Green building compliance, real-time air quality monitoring stations, indoor air quality monitoring in public buildings |
| **Wastewater** | 5 | Treated wastewater reuse, smart sensor monitoring of pipeline network |
| **Finance** | 2 | Sharing economy revenue, electronic payment percentage |
| **Population & Social** | 4 | Public building accessibility for persons with special needs, digital divide bridging budget |
| **Education** | 3 | Digital learning devices per 1,000 students, STEM degrees per 100,000 population |
| **Economy** | 4 | Open data policy in service contracts, ICT sector employment, new business survival rate |
| **Recreation** | 1 | Online booking availability for public recreation services |
| **Sport & Culture** | 4 | Online cultural facility bookings, digitized cultural records, active public library users |
| **Urban Agriculture** | 3 | Municipal budget for urban agriculture, food waste composting, online food-supplier mapping |

### Key Differences

| Dimension | ISO 37120 | ISO 37122 |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Purpose** | Baseline sustainability and service quality | Smart/digital maturity assessment |
| **Indicators** | 104 (basic services) | 81 (technology-smart layers) |
| **Transportation focus** | Public transport availability, length of network | Real-time systems, autonomous vehicles, smart traffic lights, unified payments |
| **Energy focus** | Grid efficiency, consumption per capita | Smart meters, decentralized production, EV infrastructure, waste-to-energy |
| **Governance focus** | Voter turnout, financial transparency | Open data portal usage, online service accessibility, IT downtime |
| **Data type** | Mostly outcome indicators (52%) | Mostly output indicators measuring technology presence |

### Governance Model
- **Voluntary certification** through World Council on City Data (WCCD)
- **Three certification levels**: Platinum (90+ indicators), Gold, Silver
- **Independent third-party verification** required [^113^]
- Cities report annually with standardized methodologies enabling cross-city comparison

### Citizen Engagement
- Both standards include governance indicators (voter turnout, public information access)
- ISO 37122 specifically tracks: citizens engaged in planning process per 100,000 population, online visits to open data portals, accessibility of city services online [^81^]

### How They Measure "Smart" Differently
ISO 37120 measures whether a city provides **quality basic services** — the foundation of sustainability. ISO 37122 measures whether those services are **digitally enhanced and intelligently managed**. ISO 37120 asks "Do citizens have access to public transport?" while ISO 37122 asks "Is that transport equipped with real-time tracking, unified payment, and internet connectivity for commuters?" [^40^].

### Referencable Content
- ISO 37120:2018 full standard with indicator definitions and methodologies
- ISO 37122:2019 full standard (81 indicators across 20 sectors) [^81^][^83^]
- WCCD Certification process documentation [^113^]
- U4SSC (United for Smart Sustainable Cities) KPI methodology [^80^]
- Mapping annexes connecting indicators to SDGs and ISO 37101 [^83^]

## 3. ASEAN Smart Cities Framework (ASCF)

### Overview
The ASEAN Smart Cities Framework (ASCF) was adopted at the 33rd ASEAN Summit in November 2018 to guide the **ASEAN Smart Cities Network (ASCN)** — a collaborative platform of 26 pilot cities (expanded to 35+ by 2025) across all 10 ASEAN Member States [^109^][^119^]. The framework serves as a guide to facilitate smart city development without imposing uniform standards on diverse national contexts.

### Core Framework Structure

The ASCF is organized around:
- **6 Strategic Outcomes**: Civic & Social, Health & Well-Being, Safety & Security, Quality Environment, Built Infrastructure, Industry & Innovation [^114^]
- **Key Urban Systems**: Energy, Mobility, Water & Waste Management, Digital Infrastructure, Governance
- **Enablers**: Policy & Regulation, Partnerships & Finance, Technology & Data, Capacity Building

### KPIs and Monitoring

The ASCN uses an annual **Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) framework** structured around six pillars [^114^]:

| Pillar | Focus Areas | Example Metrics |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Civic & Social** | Citizen participation, inclusivity, social cohesion | Citizen participation rate, community engagement satisfaction, social cohesion and community trust |
| **Health & Well-Being** | Healthcare access, public health | Population with online unified health files, remote medical appointments |
| **Safety & Security** | Crime prevention, emergency response | Crime prevention and public safety index, emergency response efficiency, cybersecurity incident response |
| **Quality Environment** | Sustainability, climate resilience | Renewable energy integration rate, smart grid deployment, climate resilience planning |
| **Built Infrastructure** | Transport, utilities, digital | Interoperability of city systems, smart infrastructure adaptability index, traffic congestion reduction |
| **Industry & Innovation** | Digital economy, entrepreneurship | Entrepreneurial ecosystem strength, number of startups/incubators, innovative solutions addressing inequalities |

### Governance Model
- **Multi-layered**: National governments nominate cities; cities develop Smart City Action Plans (SCAPs); ASEAN Secretariat coordinates
- **Voluntary, non-binding**: Framework serves as guide, complementing existing national development plans
- **Peer-to-peer learning**: Annual high-level meetings, thematic roundtables, ASCN Discussion Series [^114^]
- **External partnerships**: 33+ partnerships with external partners including U.S., Japan, China, Australia for capacity building and funding [^109^]

### Citizen Engagement Mechanisms
- **Participatory planning**: Citizens engaged in planning process (tracked as indicator)
- **Co-creation workshops**: Working with citizens directly to identify pivots and modifications
- **Digital engagement platforms**: Public consultations, community forums
- **Community trust surveys**: Social cohesion and community trust metrics [^60^]

### Identified Gaps and Challenges
1. **Divergent development levels**: Cities range from advanced (Singapore) to developing (Vientiane, Battambang), creating implementation asymmetries [^111^]
2. **Human rights concerns**: Policy briefs note potential negligence of civic and social rights, with illiberal governance models in some member states potentially conflicting with smart city inclusivity goals [^111^]
3. **External funding dependency**: Risk of "developing models" from financing stakeholders (Japan, China) imposing priorities rather than local needs [^111^]
4. **No binding standards**: Framework is voluntary with no enforcement mechanism
5. **Data availability uncertainty**: Many cities lack baseline data collection capacity [^40^]

### How It Measures "Smart" Differently
The ASCF uniquely emphasizes **technology as an enabler of people-centered development** rather than an end in itself. It adopts an inclusive approach "respectful of human rights and fundamental freedoms as inscribed in the ASEAN Charter" [^109^]. The framework also emphasizes **cultural contextuality** — smart city solutions must be adapted to diverse ASEAN cultural, political, and economic contexts rather than imported wholesale.

### Referencable Content
- **ASEAN Smart Cities Framework** (full document adopted at 33rd ASEAN Summit) [^109^]
- **ASEAN Smart City Planning Guidebook** (2022): Standardized frameworks and practical examples for developing integrated smart city strategies [^114^][^45^]
- **ASCN Monitoring & Evaluation Reports**: Annual tracking of 77+ projects across 6 pillars [^114^]
- **Smart City Action Plans (SCAPs)**: City-specific documents outlining vision, priority areas, strategic targets, and projects for all 26 pilot cities [^109^]
- **ASEAN Smart City Investment Toolkit** (under development with Australian Government support) [^71^]

## 4. EU Smart Cities Marketplace

### Overview
The Smart Cities Marketplace is an initiative of the **European Commission's Directorate-General for Energy (DG ENER)**, established in 2019 through the merger of the Smart Cities Information System (SCIS) and the Marketplace of the European Innovation Partnership on Smart Cities and Communities (EIP-SCC) [^111^][^112^]. It functions as a matchmaking platform connecting cities, investors, and solution providers to accelerate climate-neutral urban development.

### Core Services and Tools

The Marketplace operates a **three-phase project development model** [^87^][^111^]:

| Phase | Purpose | Activities |
|-------|---------|------------|
| **Explore** | See and learn what's next | Access knowledge base, solution booklets, success stories, best practices database |
| **Shape** | Shape project and action plans | One-to-one expert consulting, feasibility enhancement, bankable project development |
| **Deal** | Create relations and opportunities | Matchmaking with investor network, one-to-one exchange to close financing |

### Available Resources and Templates

1. **Solution Booklets** (topic-specific guides):
   - Urban Freight Logistics
   - Energy Communities
   - Heat Pump driven District Heating
   - Electric Vehicles & the Grid
   - Smart Solutions for CO2 Reduction
   - Building Integrated PV
   - Citizen Engagement
   - Citizen Engagement and Positive Energy Districts [^86^]

2. **Smart Cities Guidance Package**:
   - Full guidance document for replication and scaling
   - Summary version for quick reference
   - Covers technical, financial, regulatory, and social aspects [^118^]

3. **Smart Solutions Casebook**: Case studies from cities working with private/financial partners [^86^]

4. **Green Cities Wiki**: Comprehensive roadmap with practical knowledge and expert insights [^115^]

5. **Matchmaking Services**:
   - Free technical assistance
   - Financing masterclasses
   - 1-to-1 consulting for city-led consortia
   - Investor Network access for mature projects [^113^]

### KPIs and Impact Metrics

| Metric | Value (as of 2023) |
|--------|-------------------|
| Bankable project proposals received | 130+ projects |
| Aggregated investment value | EUR 668+ million |
| Projects matched with investors | ~130 projects |
| Focus on small/mid-sized cities | Primary target (<100,000 inhabitants) |
| EU Member States covered | All 27 + Horizon Europe-eligible countries |

### Governance Model
- **EU Commission-led**: Managed by DG ENER with EUR 6+ million budget
- **Consortium delivery**: VITO/EnergyVille (lead), Technopolis Group, DNV, Steinbeis Europa Zentrum, ICLEI, and others [^112^]
- **City-led projects**: Projects must be led by or include at least one city
- **Continuous intake**: Applications accepted year-round, assessed personally [^110^]

### Citizen Engagement Mechanisms
- **Citizen Engagement Solution Booklet**: Dedicated guidance on citizen participation methods
- **Participatory approaches**: Featured in project requirements and best practices
- **Positive Energy Districts booklet**: Community-centered energy transition guidance [^86^]

### How It Measures "Smart" Differently
The EU Marketplace uniquely defines "smart" through a **financing and investment lens**. A project is "smart" not just because it uses technology, but because it is **bankable, scalable, and investment-grade**. The platform prioritizes projects that can attract private capital and demonstrate return on investment. It also emphasizes **climate neutrality** as the primary outcome — smart cities are climate-neutral cities [^115^].

### Referencable Content
- **Smart Cities Guidance Package** (full + summary versions) [^118^]
- **Smart Solutions Casebook** (2022) [^86^]
- **Solution Booklets** (8+ topic-specific guides)
- **Green Cities Wiki** — comprehensive knowledge base [^115^]
- **Matchmaking intake form and guidance document** [^110^]
- **SCIS (Smart Cities Information System)** database of 550+ innovative urban solutions from 124 cities [^115^]
- **Replication guides** for scaling successful projects across European cities [^118^]

## 5. Singapore Smart Nation Framework

### Overview
Singapore's Smart Nation initiative, launched in 2014 and evolving into **Smart Nation 2.0**, represents one of the world's most comprehensive national digital transformation programs. It is guided by the **Digital Government Blueprint (DGB)** and led by the Smart Nation and Digital Government Group (SNDGG), with the Government Technology Agency (GovTech) and Open Government Products (OGP) as key implementation bodies [^62^][^69^].

### Digital Government Blueprint KPIs

The DGB establishes 15 specific KPIs with 2023 targets [^69^]:

| Category | KPI | Target (By 2023) |
|----------|-----|------------------|
| **Stakeholder Satisfaction** | Citizen satisfaction with digital services | 75-80% "very satisfied" |
| **Stakeholder Satisfaction** | Business satisfaction with digital services | 75-80% "very satisfied" |
| **End-to-End Digital Options** | Services with e-payment options | 100% |
| **End-to-End Digital Options** | Services pre-filled with government-verified data | 100% |
| **End-to-End Digital Options** | Services with digital signature options | 100% |
| **Digital Transactions** | Transactions completed end-to-end digitally | 90-95% |
| **Digital Transactions** | Payments via e-payments | 100% |
| **Digital Capabilities** | Public officers trained in data analytics/science | 20,000 |
| **Digital Capabilities** | Public officers with basic digital literacy | All officers |
| **Transformative Projects** | Number of transformative digital projects | 30-50 projects |
| **AI & Data Analytics** | Ministry families using AI for service delivery/policy | All ministries (at least 1 AI project each) |
| **AI & Data Analytics** | High-impact cross-agency data analytics projects | 10/year + 2 per ministry family/year |
| **AI & Data Analytics** | Core data fields in machine-readable API format | 90-100% |
| **AI & Data Analytics** | Time to share data for cross-agency projects | Max 7 working days |
| **Cloud Migration** | Eligible government systems on commercial cloud | 70% |

### Additional Investment KPIs [^70^]
- **WEF GII (Global Innovation Index) Ranking**: Benchmark against global peers
- **e-Government Ranking**: Track digital government performance
- **Productivity Growth**: Economic impact of digitalization
- **Online Services Usage**: Population-level adoption metrics
- **Net New IT Jobs**: Employment in ICT sector
- **ICT Employment Rate**: Workforce digital skills

### Governance Model
- **Centralized top-down with agile delivery**: SNDGG sets national strategy; GovTech implements; OGP drives innovation
- **"Digital to the Core, Serves with Heart"**: Aspiration statement guiding approach [^62^]
- **Digital Maturity Index**: Assesses digital maturity across public sector agencies, benchmarked against private sector [^69^]
- **Whole-of-government**: Cross-agency data sharing mandated (max 7 days); 10 cross-agency analytics projects/year required [^69^]
- **AI governance**: All ministry families required to have at least one AI project by 2023 [^69^]

### Citizen Engagement Mechanisms
- **Build for Good (BFG)**: Month-long citizen hackathon where citizens identify problems, ideate solutions, build prototypes, and test with users. Winning teams receive 8-week accelerator with funding [^116^]
- **LifeSG app**: Citizen-centric platform aggregating government services
- **Singpass**: National digital identity enabling seamless service access
- **Open Government Products**: Experimental unit creating citizen-focused digital products

### How It Measures "Smart" Differently
Singapore measures "smart" through **transaction completion rates and citizen satisfaction scores** rather than infrastructure deployment alone. The emphasis on "end-to-end digital" transactions (90-95% target) reflects a focus on **frictionless citizen experience**. The Digital Maturity Index explicitly benchmarks government against private sector standards, treating government as a service provider competing for citizen preference [^69^]. The Build for Good program also represents a unique model of **citizen-driven innovation** rather than government-led technology push.

### Referencable Content
- **Digital Government Blueprint** (public document with full KPI table) [^69^]
- **Smart Nation 2.0 Report** [^116^]
- **GovTech Playbooks & Guides**: Practical frameworks for designing, delivering, and sustaining digital services [^62^]
- **Build for Good program documentation**: Citizen engagement model [^116^]
- **Open-source tools and national apps**: Technical resources shared publicly [^62^]

## 6. South Korea K-City Testing Framework

### Overview
K-City is South Korea's **autonomous vehicle testing and smart city simulation facility** — the world's largest of its kind when opened. It represents a unique "testbed-first" approach to smart city development, where technologies are validated in controlled environments before public deployment. The project reflects South Korea's broader strategy of regulatory reform combined with physical testing infrastructure [^61^][^63^].

### Technical Specifications

| Feature | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| **Total area** | 320,000 square meters |
| **Budget** | KRW 12.5 billion (~USD $11-19.9 million including private funds) |
| **Location** | Hwaseong, southwest of Seoul (Korea Transportation Safety Authority) |
| **Testing environments** | 5 major zones: highway, downtown road, suburban street, parking lot, community facilities |
| **Simulated conditions** | Up to 35 different scenarios |
| **Network** | 5G, 4G, V2X (Vehicle-to-Everything) deployed |
| **Autonomous levels tested** | Up to Level 4 (fully autonomous in constrained environments) |

### Five Testing Environments [^63^]
1. **Highway**: High-speed autonomous driving, merging, lane changing
2. **Downtown road**: Complex urban scenarios with pedestrians, intersections
3. **Suburban street**: Mixed traffic, lower speed, residential patterns
4. **Parking lot**: Automated parking, low-speed maneuvering
5. **Community facilities**: Specific use-case environments

### 35 Simulated Conditions Include [^61^]
- Zebra crossings, toll gates, train track crossings
- Road works and construction zones
- Various weather and lighting conditions
- Emergency scenarios and accident simulations
- V2X communication testing

### Governance Model
- **Government-led consortium**: Korea Automobile Testing and Research Institute (KATRI) coordinates
- **16-partner consortium**: Includes Hyundai Motor Group, Samsung Electronics, SK Telecom, Korean OEMs, suppliers, and research institutes [^63^][^68^]
- **Regulatory sandbox approach**: Korean government took regulatory changes to allow autonomous vehicle testing and set standards for Level 3 vehicles [^64^]
- **Open to international collaboration**: K-City is open to European researchers and institutes [^68^]
- **Timeline**: 2016-2019 (Phase 1); ongoing expansion

### KPIs and Measurement Approach
- **Safety verification**: Primary KPI is safety assessment and validation
- **Technology readiness levels**: Progress through SAE automation levels (0-5)
- **Scenario coverage**: Number of simulated conditions (target: 35+)
- **Network performance**: 5G latency, V2X communication reliability
- **Industry participation**: Number of consortium partners (16+)

### How It Measures "Smart" Differently
K-City defines "smart" through a **safety verification and technology validation lens** rather than outcome metrics. The fundamental question is: "Can autonomous systems operate safely across diverse scenarios before deployment?" This is a **pre-deployment testing model** unlike other frameworks that measure deployed system performance. It treats the city itself as a **living laboratory** where risk can be managed through controlled simulation [^61^][^68^].

### Citizen Engagement
- **Public demonstration events**: Real-world autonomous driving experiences for citizens and businesses
- **Industry-academia-government collaboration**: Multi-stakeholder involvement in testing
- **Regulatory co-development**: Standards developed with industry input [^63^]

### Referencable Content
- **K-City technical documentation**: Test site specifications and capabilities [^68^]
- **Automated Vehicle Test-bed reports**: Safety assessment methodologies
- **Consortium partnership models**: Multi-stakeholder governance structure
- **Regulatory framework for autonomous vehicles**: Korean standards for Level 3 deployment [^64^]
- **3D high-definition mapping standards**: National traffic sign harmonization approaches [^64^]

## 7. Japan's Society 5.0 City Implementation

### Overview
Society 5.0 is Japan's national vision for a "human-centered society that achieves economic development and resolves social issues by mixing together the cyber realm (virtual space) and the physical realm (real space) at an advanced level" [^67^]. It was defined in the 5th Science and Technology Basic Plan (2016) and positions smart cities as **leading demonstration opportunities** for Society 5.0 principles.

### Relationship Between Society 5.0, SDGs, and Smart Cities
Japan's framework creates an explicit formula: **"Social issues identified in the SDGs" x "Solving social issues through technology" = "Realization of smart cities"** [^65^]. Smart cities are positioned as the physical implementation layer of Society 5.0, which itself operationalizes the SDGs through technology.

### Three Pillars of Japanese Smart Cities [^66^]
1. **Eco-Cities** (environmentally symbiotic cities)
2. **TOD** (Transit-Oriented Development)
3. **Disaster-Resilient Cities** (resilience as core feature)

### Smart City Model Projects
The Japanese government held a public competition for consortiums of private businesses and local governments, selecting **15 "Leading Model Projects"** to pioneer national smart city initiatives [^65^]. These projects focus on:

| Priority Area | Technology Applications |
|---------------|------------------------|
| Disaster prevention and mitigation | IoT sensors, AI prediction, early warning systems |
| Elderly care services | Monitoring systems, robotics, remote healthcare |
| Child services | Safety monitoring, smart childcare |
| Transportation | 3D location analysis, automated driving, drones, MaaS |
| Healthcare enhancement | Telemedicine, health data platforms, preventive care |
| Energy efficiency | Smart grids, decentralized renewable energy |
| Zero emissions | Carbon tracking, clean energy integration |
| Infection control | Smart health monitoring, public health surveillance |

### KPIs and Measurement
- **Horizontal deployment metrics**: Number of cities replicating model project findings
- **Guidelines publication**: Model project findings compiled as national guidelines
- **Public-private partnerships established**: Number of consortiums formed
- **Citizen expectation alignment**: Annual surveys track top 5 expected service areas [^65^]

### Governance Model
- **Public-private-academia collaboration**: Consortium model with government, business, and research partners
- **Cabinet Office-led**: National strategy from highest government level
- **Competitive selection**: Public competition for model project funding
- **Horizontal rollout**: Successful pilots systematically scaled nationwide through guidelines and support mechanisms [^65^]
- **Smart City Catalog**: Case study compilation for cross-city learning [^66^]

### Citizen Engagement
- **Citizen surveys**: Annual Intage Inc. surveys identify top citizen priorities (disaster prevention ranks #1) [^65^]
- **Co-creation consortiums**: Private sector and local government partnerships designed with citizen needs in mind
- **Data visualization platforms**: Cross-sectoral data collected and visualized for public transparency

### How It Measures "Smart" Differently
Japan's framework is unique in three ways: (1) It explicitly links smart cities to a **national societal vision** (Society 5.0) rather than treating them as isolated urban projects; (2) It prioritizes **disaster resilience and aging population care** — challenges specific to Japan's context — over generic smart city goals; (3) It uses a **demonstration-then-scale model** where 15 pilot projects generate guidelines for nationwide horizontal deployment [^65^][^66^].

### Referencable Content
- **Japan's Smart Cities Catalog**: "Solving the SDGs and Other Global Issues with Japan's Society 5.0" [^66^]
- **Smart City model project guidelines**: Compiled findings from 15 leading projects [^65^]
- **Smart City-ASEAN cooperation reports**: Japan-ASEAN urban development collaboration [^66^]
- **Cabinet Office Society 5.0 documentation**: National strategy papers
- **Case studies**: Specific project examples (Yokohama, Kashiwa-no-ha, Sapporo, etc.)

## 8. Dubai 10X / Smart Dubai

### Overview
Smart Dubai, launched in March 2014, aims to make Dubai "the happiest city on Earth." It is led by the **Smart Dubai Office** and operates under the **Dubai Data Law (2015)**. The initiative has launched **100+ smart initiatives and 1,000+ smart services** across 20+ government bodies and private sector partners [^112^]. Dubai 10X is a complementary initiative focused on making Dubai's government 10 years ahead of other cities through future-facing projects [^82^].

### Two Core Measurement Indexes

#### 1. Happiness Meter Index
- **Simple 3-point rating**: Satisfied / Neutral / Dissatisfied
- **Real-time collection**: Through smart devices at government headquarters, cellphones, computers
- **Coverage**: 1 million+ interactions across 31 government entities (as of 2016)
- **Purpose**: Centralized data dashboard feeding policy makers with citizen satisfaction data
- **Reports generated**: Sent to policy makers to improve underperforming services [^112^]

#### 2. Smart Dubai Index
Based on **ITU Smart Sustainable Cities KPIs** — Dubai was the **world's first pilot city** to apply these [^117^]:

| Dimension | Sample KPIs |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Information & Communication Technologies** | Broadband penetration, digital government service adoption, IT infrastructure uptime |
| **Environmental Sustainability** | Carbon emissions, renewable energy share, green building compliance |
| **Productivity** | Economic output per capita, business registration efficiency, innovation index |
| **Equity & Social Inclusion** | Digital divide metrics, accessibility compliance, inclusive service design |
| **Quality of Life** | Health outcomes, education access, cultural amenities, public space quality |
| **Physical Infrastructure** | Transport efficiency, utility reliability, autonomous mobility readiness |

### Smart Dubai 2021/2030 Strategic Pillars [^112^]
- **Four goals**: Seamless, Efficient, Safe, Personalized
- **Three axes**: Customer happiness, Economic growth, Infrastructure resilience
- **Six dimensions**: Smart economy, Smart governance, Smart environment, Smart people, Smart mobility, Smart living

### Additional Strategic Targets
- **25% of all mobility journeys** to be smart, self-driving by 2030 (9.4% achieved as of Feb 2024) [^82^]
- **Zero Emissions Public Transportation by 2050** [^82^]
- **Dubai Data Initiative**: Citywide data opening and sharing across public and private sectors

### Governance Model
- **Executive Office leadership**: Smart Dubai Office established in 2015, reporting to executive leadership
- **OSCIM (Office of Smart City Impact Management)**: Oversees implementation of all projects, tracks KPIs and impact indicators at city level [^117^]
- **Dubai Data Establishment**: Manages city data law implementation
- **Smart Dubai Government**: Technical implementation arm
- **Impact-driven evaluation**: All initiatives assessed across three impact areas: Customer Impact, Financial Impact, Resource & Infrastructure Impact [^117^]

### Citizen Engagement Mechanisms
- **Happiness Meter**: Real-time satisfaction tracking at every government touchpoint [^112^]
- **Happiness Snapshot**: Study collecting data on beliefs and motivations of Dubai residents to inform new projects and policies [^112^]
- **National Program for Happiness and Positivity** (2016): Three areas — inclusion of happiness in government policies/programs, promoting positivity as lifestyle, developing measurement benchmarks [^112^]
- **Centralized data dashboard**: Monitors and analyzes real-time performance across government services

### How It Measures "Smart" Differently
Dubai's approach is fundamentally unique in making **happiness the ultimate success indicator** rather than technology deployment or efficiency metrics. As noted in the framework documentation, "instead of focusing on one specific area of improvement, Dubai places the people's happiness as the heart of its strategy" [^112^]. The Happiness Meter's 3-point scale (satisfied/neutral/dissatisfied) is deliberately simple to maximize participation. Smart Dubai also pioneered the use of **ITU Smart Sustainable Cities KPIs** as the first global pilot city, establishing a template for international standard adoption at city level [^117^].
